How much does an ecommerce website cost in India?

Last updated: 21 September 2026 · A Brand Mint pricing guide

Short answer: at Brand Mint, a fixed-price online store for an Indian brand runs from ₹49,999 to ₹3,00,000, one-time, exclusive of 18% GST. That is a wide range on purpose — a WhatsApp-run store for someone just starting out and a full store with staff roles, reviews and advanced reporting are not the same build, and quoting them the same way would be dishonest. What follows is the actual breakdown, not a "starting from" number designed to get you on a call.

What actually drives the cost

Ignore the marketing copy for a second. These are the specific things that move an ecommerce build from ₹49,999 to ₹3,00,000, in the order they usually come up:

Payments and cash on delivery

Online payments need a Razorpay account in your business name, with KYC and a settlement bank account — the transaction fee is charged to you directly, not billed through the build. COD needs the opposite kind of thinking: a confirmation step to cut fake orders, and a reconciliation process for what the courier collects and remits. Most Indian stores launch with both and watch the split for the first quarter.

GST, and where it applies

The build price itself is exclusive of 18% GST — a ₹99,000 Starter Store invoices at ₹1,16,820. Separately, your own product GST rates depend on HSN code and have to be right in the catalogue from day one; retro-fixing tax on live orders is painful.

Logistics: couriers, tracking and returns

A courier API integration turns an order into a shipment automatically and gives the customer live tracking, which removes most "where is my order" support load. Returns are the part people under-plan for: you need a stated window, a condition policy, and a refund path back through your payment gateway. Courier integration, tracking and refunds start at the Growth tier for this reason.

Catalogue: your product data is the product

Search, filters, related products and every recommendation run off structured data, not photographs. Consistent categories, real variant attributes and honest stock counts do more for conversion than a redesign — and a prepared catalogue is the single biggest thing that keeps a build on schedule, since timelines run from the day the studio receives your assets.

Recovery and lifecycle email

Most people who add to cart do not check out on that visit. A recovery sequence and the ordinary lifecycle emails — confirmation, shipped, delivered — are some of the cheapest revenue in ecommerce, because the buying intent already exists. Both come in from the Growth tier up.

Measurement: GA4 and Meta Pixel, set up properly

Installing a tracking tag is not measurement. What matters is conversion events firing on the right actions with the right values, so you can see which channel actually pays for itself rather than guessing from the ad platform's own reporting.

Compliance costs that surprise first-time owners

These aren't optional extras — they're legal requirements for selling online in India, and skipping them is what gets a Razorpay account held or a store fined later:

  • GST registration and invoicing — selling online across state lines generally needs GST registration regardless of turnover, with GSTIN and HSN codes on every invoice.
  • Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 — your legal name, address, contact details and a grievance officer must be visible on the store, with complaints acknowledged within 48 hours.
  • Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) — pre-packaged goods need MRP, net quantity, manufacturer/packer/importer and country of origin shown on the product page itself, not buried in a description.
  • DPDP Act, 2023 — personal data collected only with notice and consent, for a stated purpose, with a privacy policy that reflects what the store actually does.
  • Category licences — FSSAI for food and supplements, BIS marks for certain electronics and toys, a drug licence for pharmacy, hallmarking for gold jewellery, where applicable.

This is general guidance, not legal advice — your CA or lawyer confirms what applies to your category and turnover. A studio that builds a store without these fields is handing you a liability, not a discount.
